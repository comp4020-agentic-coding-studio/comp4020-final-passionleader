# Final project reflection

## The breakthrough that moved the work forward

The plan stayed with me, and the doing went to the agents. An idea went into
my notes the moment it came, became a numbered item on the checklist, was
built by an agent, landed as a commit carrying that number, and was ticked
off with the commit beside it. Once that loop was in place, I stopped
managing the code and started managing the ideas, and the project moved
faster than I could have moved it alone.

## What this changed about who I want to be as a developer

With AI, building is now very easy. You can make the program you want and
put it on the web for anyone to open, almost in one go. The result is a
flood of data and content, and people get bored of it quickly.

So I think the easier building gets, the better you have to know the user.
What does the user really need? What gets in their way in my service? Both
come from 'human experience', a sense that AI won't find for you. If a
service doesn't answer them, it is just one more piece of the throwaway
content that AI turns out by the thousand.

So a polished, real service that earns people's attention is worth far
more than a rough prototype. I want to be a developer who 'survives':
one with the drive to push things through and the care to get the details
right.
